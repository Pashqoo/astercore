Astercore

The buttons above

Stopping the core — or restarting it — withdraws every order that could open or grow a position, and leaves the exits: between this process going away and the next one starting, nobody is watching a fill, while a position whose stop went with it would be worse off than before. Panic is still a different button — it takes the exits too, and sends the positions out. Both live in the bar at the top, reachable from every tab: a panic the operator has to go looking for is the wrong kind of button.

Profit

All three count a deal by when it closed, like the chat and the picture thresholds do; only the window differs, and «report» is every deal data/reports.jsonl still holds, however old. The terminal's own table keeps its own counter, which also counts the positions still open — these three do not.

Positions

Strategies

Edited in the terminal; the core runs what it was given.

On start

A deploy, a crash or a reboot all end in a start. «Remember» is what the core has always done — it puts back the flag the last stop left behind; «always start stopped» is the one to pick when an unplanned restart should not put the strategies back on the market by itself.

Bot

The chat is approved by the PIN the core prints in its journal, not typed in here. On the server api.telegram.org is reachable only through the local SOCKS5 proxy.

Reports

Deal pictures

Any armed threshold that is passed sends the picture; 0 turns that one off. The window is the deal itself: a minute before the entry, half a minute after the close — not a setting, and not the terminal's «time scale», which is a zoom of its own chart.

Now

Rules

Read only: these arrive with the terminal's settings and are edited there, on its «Auto start» tab. The core acts on exactly what is listed here.

Core

Account

The account is the API wallet's key file (ASTER_API_KEY_FILE, asterkey by default): the core reads it at the start and never writes it, and it is not here and never will be. Another account is another key file and a restart. Whether the exchange takes the key is the exchange chip in the header: without one the core trades in the emulator only.

This page

An address that is not loopback is refused without a password. Even with one, reach it over an ssh tunnel: the journal below goes out through this page too. A new password drops every login, this one included.

Strategy screener

    

      A strategy's own settings, loaded from the core and moved here for the asking: the table
      is what its screener would watch and what its filters would let it enter, market by market,
      measured right now. Nothing here is saved — move a corridor, watch the table, then type the
      numbers you settled on into the terminal.
      Two different questions are kept apart, because the core keeps them apart: the pool is a
      ranking — MarketTags and the dynamic lists decide which markets the strategy is
      about — and the filters are a gate on entering one of them. A market can be watched
      all day and entered never.
      
«Filters passed» is exactly that and no more: the screener and the Filters tab let this
      market through right now. Whether an order actually goes out is the rest of the pass —
      MaxMarkets, WorkingTime, the penalties, the session guards, a position already
      open — and none of it is asked here.

Limits and calls

    

      First the exchange's own counts of what this IP spent — weight a minute and new orders a minute
      and per ten seconds, from the answers' headers, against Aster's limits — then a card per call:
      the line is requests a minute, the red one is what it may spend, and the strip under it marks
      every minute that came back an error (red) or that the core did not send at all while it was
      holding for the exchange's Retry-After (amber). The line is the worst minute behind each pixel, not an average — the ceiling
      is per minute, and a smoothed line would hide the minute that reached it.
